# Modern history and independence

## Uzbek SSR
The Uzbek Soviet Socialist Republic was formed in 1924 during the Soviet national-territorial reorganization of Central Asia.

Samarkand served as the republic's capital in the 1920s before the capital was moved to Tashkent in 1930.

## Soviet modernization
Soviet rule expanded mass education, industrialization, transportation and urban development. It also introduced strong state control over politics, religion, culture, agriculture and publishing.

## Cotton and the Aral Sea
Uzbekistan became one of the Soviet Union's major cotton-producing regions. Extensive irrigation projects diverted water from the Amu Darya and Syr Darya systems.

The reduction of water reaching the Aral Sea contributed to one of the world's major environmental disasters.

## Stalinist repression
During the 1930s, many Uzbek and Central Asian intellectuals, including prominent writers and Jadids, were arrested, imprisoned or executed during Stalinist purges.

## Independence
In 1991, Uzbekistan became an independent state following the collapse of the Soviet Union. Independence Day is celebrated on 1 September.

## Language after independence
After independence, Uzbekistan expanded the public role of Uzbek and adopted a Latin-based Uzbek alphabet by law in 1993, later revised in 1995.
