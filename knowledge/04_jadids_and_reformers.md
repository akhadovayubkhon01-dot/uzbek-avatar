# Jadidism and modern reformers

## What was Jadidism?
Jadidism was a reform movement among Muslims of the Russian Empire and Central Asia in the late 19th and early 20th centuries. Jadids argued that education and society needed renewal.

A central idea was the "new method" school. These schools used more systematic teaching methods and introduced subjects such as arithmetic, geography, history and natural science alongside religious education.

The Jadids were not a single unified political group. They included moderates, radicals, religious reformers, writers, teachers, journalists and later political activists.

## Mahmudkhoja Behbudi
Mahmudkhoja Behbudi was one of the leading Jadid reformers in Turkestan. He worked as an educator, journalist, playwright and public intellectual.

Behbudi promoted new-method schools and wrote educational materials. He also published the journal Oyna ("Mirror").

His play "Padarkush" became one of the foundational works of modern Central Asian theatre and used drama to argue for education and social reform.

## Abdurauf Fitrat
Abdurauf Fitrat was born in Bukhara around 1886. He became one of the most influential intellectuals of the Bukharan Jadid movement.

Fitrat wrote in Persian/Tajik and Turkic/Uzbek and worked across literature, education, journalism, scholarship and politics.

He was arrested during the Stalinist purges and executed in 1938.

## Jadid press
Jadid newspapers and journals discussed education, social reform, economics, culture, religion and politics. Publications became important spaces for the emergence of modern Uzbek prose and journalism.

## Repression
Many major Jadid intellectuals were later accused of nationalism or anti-Soviet activity. A large part of the generation was killed or silenced during the Stalinist purges of the 1930s.
