# Scholars, writers and historians connected to the region

Important: many people below lived centuries before modern Uzbek national identity developed. It is more accurate to describe them by their city, dynasty, language or region than to call all of them "Uzbek."

## Muhammad al-Khwarizmi
Muhammad al-Khwarizmi was a 9th-century mathematician and astronomer associated by name with Khwarazm. He worked in Baghdad at the Abbasid court's scholarly centre.

His mathematical writings helped transmit methods for calculation and algebra. The word "algorithm" ultimately derives from the Latinized form of his name, while "algebra" comes from the title of one of his best-known works.

## Abu Rayhan al-Biruni
Abu Rayhan al-Biruni was born in 973 in the Khwarazm region. He became one of the great polymaths of the medieval Islamic world.

He wrote on astronomy, mathematics, geography, chronology, mineralogy and the cultures of India.

## Ibn Sina / Avicenna
Ibn Sina, known in Latin Europe as Avicenna, was born in 980 near Bukhara. He became a philosopher, physician and scholar.

His "Canon of Medicine" became one of the most influential medical texts of the medieval period.

## Imam al-Bukhari
Muhammad ibn Ismail al-Bukhari was born in Bukhara in the 9th century. He became one of the most famous scholars of hadith in Sunni Islam.

His collection commonly known as Sahih al-Bukhari is one of the most influential hadith compilations in Sunni scholarship.

## Al-Tirmidhi
Muhammad al-Tirmidhi was a 9th-century hadith scholar associated with the area of Termez. His Jami al-Tirmidhi became one of the major hadith collections used in Sunni scholarship.

## Narshakhi
Muhammad ibn Jafar al-Narshakhi was a 10th-century historian from the Bukhara region. He wrote an early history of Bukhara in Arabic.

The original Arabic text has not survived, but the work became famous through a Persian translation and later revisions.

## Alisher Navoi
Alisher Navoi was born in Herat in 1441 and became one of the most influential literary figures of the Timurid world. He wrote extensively in Chagatai Turkic and Persian.

Navoi's prestige helped establish Chagatai as a major literary language. Modern Uzbek literary culture regards him as a foundational classical author.

## Zahiriddin Muhammad Babur
Babur was born in Andijan in 1483 into the Timurid dynasty. He later founded the Mughal Empire in northern India in 1526.

Babur wrote the Baburnama in Chagatai Turkic. It contains observations about politics, warfare, geography, plants, cities, people and court life.

## Abdulla Qodiriy
Abdulla Qodiriy was a major figure in early modern Uzbek prose. His novel "O'tkan kunlar" is widely regarded as one of the foundational works of the modern Uzbek novel.

He was executed during the Stalinist purges in 1938.

## Cho'lpon
Abdulhamid Cho'lpon was an Uzbek poet, writer, translator and intellectual active in the early Soviet period.

He was also killed during the Stalinist purges in 1938.
