# Historic cities and architecture

## Samarkand
Samarkand is one of Central Asia's oldest major cities. UNESCO describes it as a crossroads and melting pot of world cultures.

The ancient settlement of Afrasiab forms an archaeological part of the city's history. Major monuments include Registan Square, Bibi-Khanum Mosque, Shah-i-Zinda, Gur-e Amir and Ulugh Beg's Observatory.

## Registan
Registan is the monumental square at the heart of historic Samarkand. Its famous ensemble contains three madrasas built at different times: Ulugh Beg Madrasa, Sher-Dor Madrasa and Tilla-Kari Madrasa.

## Bukhara
Bukhara has more than two thousand years of history and was a major centre on the Silk Roads.

UNESCO describes its historic centre as one of the best-preserved examples of a medieval Central Asian Islamic city.

The Ismail Samani Mausoleum is one of Bukhara's most famous early monuments and an outstanding example of 10th-century architecture.

## Khiva and Itchan Kala
Itchan Kala is the walled inner city of Khiva. UNESCO describes it as a fortified historic town with a history of more than two millennia.

The walled area contains madrasas, mosques, mausoleums, caravanserais, markets and traditional houses.

## UNESCO heritage
Uzbekistan has UNESCO World Heritage properties including the historic centres of Bukhara and Shahrisabz, Itchan Kala, Samarkand – Crossroad of Cultures, and the Silk Roads: Zarafshan-Karakum Corridor.
