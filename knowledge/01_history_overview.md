# History of the territory of present-day Uzbekistan

## Ancient and early medieval background
The territory of present-day Uzbekistan has long connected settled oasis civilizations, steppe peoples, and long-distance trade routes. Samarkand, Bukhara, Khiva, Termez and other cities developed at different times as political, commercial, religious and intellectual centres.

Samarkand grew from the ancient settlement known as Afrasiab. UNESCO describes the historic city as a major crossroads of cultures with more than two and a half millennia of history.

## Arab conquest and Islamic period
Arab armies entered Transoxiana in the 7th and 8th centuries, and Islam gradually became deeply rooted in the region. Bukhara later became one of the most important centres of Islamic scholarship in Central Asia.

## Samanid period
The Samanids ruled much of Central Asia in the 9th and 10th centuries. Bukhara was one of the dynasty's major political and cultural centres and became famous for scholarship, literature and religious learning.

## Mongol conquest
In 1220, Mongol forces under Chinggis Khan captured major cities including Bukhara and Samarkand. The conquest caused major destruction, but urban and cultural life later recovered.

## Timurid period
In the late 14th century, Timur made Samarkand the capital of a powerful empire. The Timurid period became especially important for architecture, manuscript culture, astronomy, mathematics and the arts.

## Later states
From the early 16th century, Shaybanid rulers established power in much of Transoxiana. In later centuries, the region included the Khanate of Khiva, the Emirate of Bukhara and the Khanate of Kokand.

## Russian conquest
Russian forces captured Tashkent in 1865. The Khanate of Kokand was abolished in 1876, while Bukhara and Khiva continued for a time as protectorates under strong Russian influence.

## Soviet period
The Uzbek Soviet Socialist Republic was created in 1924. Soviet rule transformed education, industry, agriculture, language policy and urban life. Cotton production expanded enormously, while large-scale irrigation contributed to the later environmental crisis of the Aral Sea.

## Independence
Uzbekistan became independent in 1991. Independence Day is celebrated on 1 September.
