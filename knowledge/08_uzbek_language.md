# Uzbek language

## Language family
Uzbek is a Turkic language. It belongs to the Karluk branch of the Turkic language family and is closely related historically and linguistically to Uyghur.

## Historical literary background
For centuries, Chagatai Turkic served as a major written literary language of Central Asia. Writers such as Alisher Navoi and Babur used Chagatai.

Modern Uzbek literature developed partly from this Chagatai literary heritage.

## Grammar
Uzbek is generally agglutinative: grammatical information is often added through sequences of suffixes.

A common basic word order is subject-object-verb, although real Uzbek sentences can vary for emphasis and style.

Uzbek does not have grammatical gender comparable to languages such as Russian, French or German.

## Vocabulary
Uzbek has a Turkic grammatical core but contains many loanwords from Persian, Arabic, Russian and, more recently, international English-based vocabulary.

## Writing systems
Uzbek has been written in several scripts during its history. Arabic script was historically important for Chagatai and earlier literary traditions.

A Latin-based alphabet was used from 1929 to 1940. During the Soviet period, Uzbek was then written mainly in Cyrillic.

Independent Uzbekistan adopted a Latin-based Uzbek alphabet by law in 1993. The law was revised in 1995 to the modern system with 26 letters and 3 letter combinations.

Cyrillic Uzbek remains widely readable and is still encountered in books, signs, media and everyday writing.

## Modern Latin alphabet features
Modern Uzbek Latin writing includes distinctive forms such as Oʻ/oʻ and Gʻ/gʻ, as well as the combinations Sh/sh, Ch/ch and Ng/ng.
