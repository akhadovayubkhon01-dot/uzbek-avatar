# Timurid era

## Amir Temur
Amir Temur, known in many Western sources as Timur or Tamerlane, was born in 1336 near Kesh, the area of modern Shahrisabz. He rose to power in Transoxiana and established Samarkand as the main capital of his empire.

Timur's empire stretched across large parts of Central Asia, Iran, Afghanistan, the Caucasus and neighbouring regions. His campaigns were often destructive, while his court also concentrated craftsmen, scholars and architects in Samarkand.

Timur died in 1405. The Gur-e Amir mausoleum in Samarkand became his burial place and one of the best-known monuments associated with the dynasty.

## Samarkand under the Timurids
UNESCO identifies the 14th and 15th centuries as Samarkand's most significant period of development. Major monuments include Bibi-Khanum Mosque, Shah-i-Zinda, Gur-e Amir, Ulugh Beg's madrasa and Ulugh Beg's observatory.

## Ulugh Beg
Ulugh Beg, a grandson of Timur, was a Timurid ruler and astronomer. He governed Samarkand and became famous for supporting mathematics and astronomy.

Ulugh Beg founded a major observatory in Samarkand in the 15th century. Astronomers working there produced highly accurate observations of stars and planetary motion for their time.

Ulugh Beg also built a madrasa on the Registan.
